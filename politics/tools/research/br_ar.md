# Research note — Brazil & Argentina (relationship)

Checked 2026-09-30. Every fact in `units/br_ar.js` traces to the sources below (cited per briefing in the unit).
Scope: South America's two giants: rivals who gave up a nuclear race and built Mercosur together, and whose presidents now barely speak. Brazil's election is in br-7; Milei's policies in ar-5.

## Rivals who gave up the bomb (`br_ar-1`)
- 1825–28: War over the Banda Oriental ends with an independent Uruguay
- 1864–70: Allies with Uruguay in the war against Paraguay
- 1970s: Secret nuclear programmes and a dispute over the Itaipu dam
- 1979: Itaipu–Corpus agreement settles the river dispute
- 1985: Alfonsín and Sarney sign the Foz do Iguaçu declaration
- 1991: ABACC: a joint agency to inspect each other's nuclear sites

- Source: [War on the Rocks: Nuclear Diplomacy Between Brazil and Argentina: An Imperfect But Important History Lesson](https://warontherocks.com/2018/05/nuclear-diplomacy-between-brazil-and-argentina-an-imperfect-but-important-history-lesson/)
- Source: [Arms Control Association: Looking Back: Lessons From the Denuclearization of Brazil and Argentina](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2006-04/looking-back-lessons-denuclearization-brazil-and-argentina)

## Mercosur and its discontents (`br_ar-2`)
- Founded: 26 March 1991, Treaty of Asunción
- Founders: Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay (Bolivia joined later)
- Common external tariff: Since 1995, with many exceptions
- EU deal: Signed 17 January 2026; provisionally in force from 1 May 2026
- Tension: Argentina's separate tariff deal with the US, February 2026
