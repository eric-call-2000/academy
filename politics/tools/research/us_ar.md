# Research note — United States & Argentina (relationship)

Checked 2026-10-01. Every fact in `units/us_ar.js` traces to the sources below (cited per briefing in the unit).
Scope: The US and Argentina: Braden or Perón, the junta, the Falklands and Menem's 'carnal relations'; the 2001 default, the holdouts, Griesa and record IMF loans; Trump's $20 billion rescue, the trade deal and the Andes-Atlantic Corridor. The midterm is in ar-6.

## Braden, the Falklands and 'carnal relations' (`us_ar-1`)
- 1945: Ambassador Spruille Braden campaigns against Perón
- Feb 1946: Perón wins with 52%: 'Braden or Perón'
- 1976–83: US first backs, then criticises, the military junta
- Apr 1982: Haig's shuttle diplomacy; the US then backs Britain
- 1990: Menem's minister speaks of 'carnal relations'
- 1998: Argentina named a major non-NATO ally

- Source: [Office of the Historian: Crisis in the South Atlantic: The Reagan Administration and the Anglo-Argentine War of 1982](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1981-1988/south-atlantic)

## Defaults, vulture funds and the IMF (`us_ar-2`)
- Dec 2001: Argentina defaults on about $100 billion
- 2005 & 2010: Debt swaps; holdouts led by Elliott refuse
- 2012: Navy ship impounded in Ghana at creditors' request
- Jul 2014: Judge Griesa's ruling pushes Argentina into default
- 2016: Macri settles with the holdouts
- 2018: Record IMF loan, backed by Trump
- Source: [Harvard Political Review: When the Holdouts Hurt](https://harvardpolitics.com/argentina-holdouts-hurt/)
- Source: [Council on Foreign Relations: Argentina's Struggle for Stability](https://www.cfr.org/backgrounders/argentinas-struggle-stability)
- Source: [Fortune: Argentina debt settlement](https://fortune.com/2016/02/29/argentina-debt-settlement)
- Source: [Congressional Research Service: Argentina: Overview and U.S. Relations](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48303)
